A city that never stops

Everything you do ripples out

Nobody here waits for the player. The city wakes up, goes to work and heads home on its own. When you step in, it reacts, and the effect spreads from street to street.

  1. You double-park your car
  2. The street jams
  3. The bus takes a detour
  4. Someone is late for work

Routines you can learn

The corner bakery fills up at 7 a.m., the street market has its day, rush hour clogs the avenue. Watch, take notes, use it to your advantage.

Stories nobody wrote

There is no script. Situations come out of the simulation: the late delivery rider, the drizzle that turns into a storm, the chase that started over a loaf of bread.

Your way

Live by the book, catch the 6 a.m. bus and clock in on time. Or don't. The city remembers both.

What you can do

Start with nothing. Live your way.

  • On foot

    Classic wave-pattern sidewalks, crosswalks, Ibirapuera Park with its lake and bike path, and buildings that open up as you walk in: the roof fades and shows the interior.

  • Subway and buses

    Tickets, turnstiles, transfers and the ride inside the train. On the bus, pull the cord and hop off at any stop.

  • Cars, motorbikes, bicycles

    Obey the lights and one-way streets, or don't. Traffic reacts: honks, swerves, slams the brakes. Coming up: truck and semi freight, city bus routes and the Tietê bus terminal.

  • Land of drizzle

    Day and night, sudden drizzle, summer storms and flooded streets that slow traffic to a crawl.

  • Actions have consequences

    Witnesses call the police, car owners chase you and squad cars join the hunt. Cartoon violence only: "POW!" and dizzy stars.

  • A life to look after

    Home, bed, hunger, sleep, phone and inventory. To pay the bills, pick up gigs: ride-hailing driver, motorbike or bike deliveries, homemade brigadeiros and popsicles to sell on the street.

  • City Notebook

    Discover and write down the citizens' routines. Small requests pop up along the way, with no mandatory quests.

  • When the city sleeps

    Late-night street races, a tuning shop and a racetrack waiting for you. And some things only happen very late…

Made in Brazil

One person. A whole city.

  • 1developer
  • 100%made in Brazil
  • 24hof living city every in-game day

I live in São Paulo and always wanted to play a game that felt like this place: the corner bakery, the packed bus, the drizzle that shows up out of nowhere and the traffic that never sleeps.

That game didn't exist, so I started making it. Code, art, sound, the map and every citizen in the city: all made by one person, here in Brazil.

Pavement Paradise was made first for people who know São Paulo and will recognize every detail. Never been here? You're welcome too: the city will show you around.

— João Rangel, creatorGitHubLinkedIn
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When does it come out?

On January 25, 2027, São Paulo's birthday. Wishlist it on Steam as soon as the page opens: it's the best way to get notified and to help an indie game.

Which platforms?

PC, on Steam, with keyboard and mouse or a controller. Other platforms may come later.

Which languages?

Brazilian Portuguese and English, from day one.

Is there a story and quests?

There's no main storyline. The city is the story. You get light goals instead: the City Notebook, requests that pop up along the way, side gigs and optional activities.

Is it the real São Paulo?

Streets, avenues and neighborhoods have their real names, and famous buildings show up in simple shapes. No real company brands or logos.

Is there violence?

Cartoon only: dizzy stars, people tumbling and a "POW!". No blood.

How much will it cost?

The price hasn't been set yet.
